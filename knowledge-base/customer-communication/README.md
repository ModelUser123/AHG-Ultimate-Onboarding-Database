## Phone Scripts

### Answering the Phone

**Standard Greeting**:
> "Thank you for calling [Shop Name], this is [Your Name]. How may I help you today?"

**If Customer Needs to Hold**:
> "May I place you on a brief hold? I'll be right with you."

**Returning from Hold**:
> "Thank you for holding. How may I assist you?"

### Appointment Scheduling

**New Customer**:
> "I'd be happy to schedule that for you. May I have your name and phone number? And what year, make, and model is your vehicle? What concerns are you having with it today?"

**Existing Customer**:
> "Welcome back, [Customer Name]. I see you have a [Year Make Model]. What can we help you with today?"

**Confirming Appointment**:
> "I have you scheduled for [Day] at [Time] for [Service]. We'll have [Technician/our team] take a look at your [concern]. Is there anything else you'd like us to check while we have it?"

### Taking Messages

> "I'd be happy to take a message for [Technician/Advisor]. May I have your name, phone number, and a brief message? I'll make sure they get this right away."

### Price Inquiries

> "I'd be happy to help with pricing. To give you an accurate estimate, I'll need to know the year, make, and model of your vehicle. [After getting info] For that service, our price typically ranges from $X to $Y, depending on what we find during inspection. Would you like to schedule an appointment so we can give you an exact quote?"

## In-Person Communication

### Greeting Customers

**When Customer Arrives**:
> "Good morning/afternoon! Welcome to [Shop Name]. How can I help you today?"

**For Appointments**:
> "Good morning/afternoon, Mr./Ms. [Name]. We've been expecting you for your [appointment type]. Let me get your information and we'll get started right away."

### Vehicle Write-Up

**Gathering Information**:
> "I'd like to go over a few things to make sure we address all your concerns today. Can you describe what you're experiencing with your vehicle?"

**Follow-up Questions**:
- "When did you first notice this?"
- "Does it happen all the time or just sometimes?"
- "Does it happen when the car is cold, warm, or both?"
- "Have you noticed anything else unusual?"

**Setting Expectations**:
> "Based on what you've described, we'll start with a [diagnostic/inspection] to determine exactly what's causing the issue. Once we know more, I'll call you with our findings and recommendations before any work is done. Is this the best number to reach you?"

### Drop-Off

> "We'll take good care of your [vehicle]. Here's your claim ticket. We should have a diagnosis for you by [time]. Is there a preferred time to call, or can I reach you anytime?"

## Explaining Repairs

### Presenting Findings

**Structure for Explaining Issues**:

1. **What we found** (the problem)
2. **Why it matters** (the consequence)
3. **What we recommend** (the solution)
4. **What it costs** (the investment)

**Example Script**:
> "During our inspection, we found that your front brake pads are down to about 2 millimeters, which is below the safe limit. If we don't replace them soon, the metal backing will start grinding into your rotors, which would add $[X] to the repair and could affect your ability to stop safely. We recommend replacing the pads and machining the rotors. The total for this repair would be $[X]. Would you like us to go ahead with that?"

### Using Analogies

**Brakes**:
> "Think of brake pads like the eraser on a pencil. Once they wear down, you're grinding metal on metal."

**Timing Belt**:
> "The timing belt is like the conductor of an orchestra, keeping all the engine's moving parts in sync. If it breaks, those parts collide and cause major damage."

**Oil Changes**:
> "Oil is the lifeblood of your engine. As it ages, it breaks down and can't protect your engine properly."

**Battery**:
> "Your battery is like a rechargeable flashlight. Eventually, it can't hold a charge anymore."

### Explaining Technical Concepts

**To Non-Technical Customers**:
> "Without getting too technical, what's happening is... [simple explanation]. The important thing to know is that this affects your [safety/reliability/efficiency]."

**When Asked for Details**:
> "I'm happy to explain more. What's happening is... [detailed explanation]. Would you like me to show you what we found?"

## Handling Objections

### "That's More Than I Expected"

> "I understand. Let me break down what's included in this estimate so you can see the value. [Explain details] We use quality parts and our work comes with a [warranty]. Is there a specific concern I can address?"

### "I Need to Think About It"

> "Absolutely, I want you to feel comfortable with your decision. Is there any additional information I can provide to help? Keep in mind that [urgency if applicable]. I'm here if you have any questions."

### "Can You Match This Price?"

> "I'd be happy to look at that quote. [Review] I notice that quote is for [different part/doesn't include X]. Our price includes [quality parts, warranty, etc.]. We're confident in our value, but let me see if there's any flexibility."

### "I'll Have My Friend/Family Do It"

> "That's understandable - I work on my own cars too when I can. Just keep in mind that [safety concern/complexity]. If you run into any issues or need parts, we're here to help."

### "Why So Expensive?"

> "I appreciate you asking. Let me explain what goes into this repair: [parts cost, labor time, expertise required, warranty]. We stand behind our work and use quality parts to ensure it's done right the first time."

### "Can You Do It Cheaper?"

> "I understand budget is important. Let me look at our estimate and see if there are any options. [Review] What we could do is [option if available]. However, I wouldn't recommend cutting corners on [safety item]."

## Delivering Bad News

### Major Unexpected Repairs

> "I have some news about your vehicle that I want to discuss with you. During our inspection, we discovered [issue]. This isn't something we expected to find, but it's important you know about it. Let me explain what this means..."

### Vehicle Not Worth Repairing

> "I've reviewed the estimate for repairs, and I want to have an honest conversation with you. The repairs total $[X], and based on your vehicle's value of approximately $[Y], you might want to consider your options. I'm not trying to make this decision for you, but I want to give you all the information."

### Delays

> "I want to update you on your vehicle. We've run into [issue], which means we'll need a bit more time than expected. I apologize for the inconvenience. Our new estimated completion time is [time]. Is there anything I can do to help in the meantime?"

### Parts Unavailability

> "I have an update on your repair. Unfortunately, the part we need is [backordered/unavailable]. Here are our options: [options]. What would you prefer?"

## Follow-Up Communication

### After Service Call

> "Hi [Customer Name], this is [Your Name] from [Shop Name]. I'm calling to follow up on the [service] we performed on your [vehicle] last week. How is everything working? ... Great to hear. If you have any questions or concerns, please don't hesitate to call. We appreciate your business!"

### Appointment Reminder

> "Hi [Customer Name], this is [Your Name] from [Shop Name] reminding you of your appointment tomorrow at [time] for [service]. If you need to reschedule, please give us a call. We look forward to seeing you!"

### Service Due Reminder

> "Hi [Customer Name], this is [Your Name] from [Shop Name]. Our records show that your [vehicle] may be due for [service]. We'd like to help you keep it running smoothly. Would you like to schedule an appointment?"

## Written Communication Templates

### Estimate Cover Letter

```
Dear [Customer Name],

Thank you for bringing your [Year Make Model] to [Shop Name] for service. 
Following our inspection, please find the attached estimate for recommended 
repairs.

Priority Items (Safety/Immediate):
- [Item 1]: $XXX
- [Item 2]: $XXX

Recommended Services:
- [Item 3]: $XXX
- [Item 4]: $XXX

Total Estimate: $X,XXX

Please note that this estimate is valid for 30 days. If you have any 
questions or would like to schedule your service, please call us at 
[phone number].

Thank you for your trust in [Shop Name].

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[Shop Name]
```

### Thank You Email/Letter

```
Dear [Customer Name],

Thank you for choosing [Shop Name] for your automotive needs. We 
appreciate your business and the opportunity to service your [vehicle].

Summary of services performed:
- [Service 1]
- [Service 2]

Your next recommended service: [Service] at [mileage/date]

If you have any questions about your service or notice anything unusual, 
please don't hesitate to contact us at [phone number].

We value your feedback! If you were satisfied with your service, we'd 
appreciate a review on [Google/Yelp].

Thank you again for your trust in our team.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Shop Name]
```

### Complaint Response

```
Dear [Customer Name],

Thank you for bringing your concerns to our attention. We take all 
feedback seriously and are committed to making this right.

I understand that [summarize complaint]. I apologize for any 
inconvenience this has caused.

To resolve this matter, [proposed solution].

Please contact me directly at [phone number] at your convenience so 
we can discuss this further.

Sincerely,
[Your Name]
[Title]
[Shop Name]
```

## Difficult Situations

### Angry Customer

**Stay Calm and Listen**:
> "I understand you're frustrated, and I want to help. Can you tell me exactly what happened?"

**Acknowledge Their Feelings**:
> "I completely understand why you'd be upset about this. Let me see what I can do to make this right."

**Offer Solutions**:
> "Here's what I can do for you... [options]. Which would work best for you?"

### Warranty Dispute

> "I understand your concern about the warranty. Let me review your service history and the warranty terms... [after review] Based on what I see, [explanation of coverage]. Here's what we can do..."

### Come-Back/Repeat Repair

> "I'm sorry you're experiencing this issue again. That's not the level of service we strive for. Let me get our [technician/manager] involved to take a fresh look at this. We want to get it right for you."

### Damage Claim

> "I want to address your concern carefully. Let me document what you're showing me and get my manager involved. We take these situations very seriously and will investigate thoroughly."

## Communication Best Practices

### Do's

- Use the customer's name
- Listen more than you talk
- Take notes on their concerns
- Explain in terms they understand
- Follow up when promised
- Be honest about what you find
- Give options when possible
- Thank them for their business

### Don'ts

- Don't talk down to customers
- Don't use excessive jargon
- Don't make promises you can't keep
- Don't argue with customers
- Don't blame other shops or technicians
- Don't dismiss their concerns
- Don't forget to follow up
